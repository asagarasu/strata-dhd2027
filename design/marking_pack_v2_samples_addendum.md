## 1. The full workflow on one line (sound, color, scalar state-change)

**蒹葭蒼蒼，白露為霜** (詩經 — pre-Han, outside all clusters)
marks: `word, repetitive` (蒼蒼) · `plant` (蒹葭) · `color, gray-green` (蒼蒼) · `wetness, small` (露) · `state_change, frozen` (為霜)

Why: the dew doesn't just sit there — the wording *turns it to frost*. When a
value changes state on the page, mark the change; that's still pointing, not
interpreting. And 蒼蒼 earns two marks (sound + color) because it does two jobs.

## 2. Emotion is legal WHEN the word carries it

**Il pleure dans mon cœur / Comme il pleut sur la ville** (Verlaine — not Baudelaire, outside the fr cluster)
marks: `emotion, weeping` (pleure) · `wetness, medium` (pleut) · `sound, echo` (pleure/pleut) · `place, city` (ville) · `body, heart` (cœur)

Why: you may mark weeping because *pleure is on the page* — the poem says weep.
Compare rule 2's abandoned-wife example: there, sadness was only in the
situation. Here it's in the wording. Same field, opposite verdicts, and the
pleure/pleut near-pun is noise — rule 4 says mark the noise.

## 3. The bright funeral (the big refusal, practiced)

**"The funeral was held on a bright morning."** (constructed)
marks: `event, funeral` · `light, bright` · `temporal, morning`

Why: the situation is sad; NO sadness mark — no sadness word exists on the page.
Yes, bright-light-at-a-funeral feels like it means something. Not your job:
record both marks and let the clash be somebody else's arithmetic. If you feel
you are "capturing the irony," stop — you're interpreting.

## 4. Scalar values come from the wording's own size

**"a thread of smoke" vs. "a pillar of smoke"** (constructed)
marks: `smoke, thin` vs. `smoke, large`

Why: same object, different value, and the value is *in the noun the author
chose*, not in your estimate of real-world smoke.

## 5. Bare fields are complete answers

**"She considered."** (constructed)
marks: `mental_action`

Why: don't force a value ("considered what? how hard?") — the wording won't
say. A bare field is a full, correct mark. Also note what we do NOT mark: the
star buried in Latin con-sīder- (pack example #2 — if you need a dictionary,
it isn't on the page).

## 6. Two pointable marks can be a whole poem

**Odi et amo.** (Catullus — no Latin cluster exists)
marks: `emotion, hate` (odi) · `emotion, love` (amo)

Why: two words, two marks, done. Do not add `conflict` or `paradox` — that's
the relation BETWEEN the marks, which the machine layer computes. You point;
the structure emerges from pointing.

## 7. Skipping, demonstrated

**Tyger Tyger, burning bright / In the forests of the night** (Blake — outside all clusters)
L1 marks: `word, repetitive` (Tyger Tyger) · `animal` (tyger) · `fire, burning` · `light, bright`
L2 marks: `plant, forest` · `temporal, night`

...and if L2 had felt like nothing but connective tissue to you, writing no
marks would ALSO be a legal answer. Three marks you can point at beat eight
you guessed; zero you can point at beats three you guessed.
