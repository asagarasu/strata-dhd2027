# Literary-Translation Reputation Claims — Verification Report

Scope: verify (or contradict, or report nothing-found for) seven reputation claims about specific translators, supplied without citations by a native-informant collaborator. Web research only, independent of the collaborator's framing. Chinese- and Japanese-language sources were searched directly; no verdict was forced where evidence was thin.

Method note: for one claim (1b, the 薔薇 anecdote) no secondary scholarly source discussing that specific poem could be found. Instead the original Chinese yuefu text and Satō Haruo's Japanese translation were located and compared directly (both are quoted primary sources, cited below). This is flagged explicitly — it is a primary-source comparison performed by me, not a citation of a scholar making the same argument.

---

## 1. Tang/classical Chinese poetry → modern Japanese (口語訳)

### 1a. 吉川幸次郎 (Yoshikawa Kōjirō) — most famous/authoritative renderer

**Verdict: CONFIRMED (general fame/authority) / UNSUPPORTED (fidelity assessment specifically)**

- His standing as top authority in Chinese literature studies in Japan is widely and explicitly stated: he is called "中国文学の最高権威者" (the highest authority in Chinese literature) and "中国文学の泰斗" (the doyen of Chinese literature studies). Setomoto Jun, 「連載 神戸秘話 17 中国文学の最高権威者 吉川幸次郎」, KOBECCO magazine, May 2018. https://kobecco.hpg.co.jp/32091/
- His co-authored `新唐詩選` (with poet Miyoshi Tatsuji, Iwanami Shinsho, 1952) is a landmark example of exactly the practice the claim describes — 口語訳 (modern colloquial rendering) of Tang poetry with running commentary, rather than 訓読 kundoku — and remains in print/widely read as a classic introduction. 吉川幸次郎・三好達治, 『新唐詩選』, 岩波新書, 1952. https://www.iwanami.co.jp/book/b267318.html
- What I could **not** find: any scholarly piece specifically assessing the *fidelity* of his renderings (i.e., debating whether his colloquial translations sacrifice precision for readability, or comparing him against kundoku-based rivals such as 目加田誠). Several targeted searches (Japanese, English) turned up only biographical/bibliographic material, not critical-methodological assessment. An English-language review by Andrew Lo of Yoshikawa's *Five Hundred Years of Chinese Poetry* (trans. J.T. Wixted) exists — *Bulletin of SOAS* 54.2 (June 1991), p. 418 — but its content on his method could not be retrieved (paywalled, abstract unavailable).

### 1b. 佐藤春夫 (Satō Haruo), 車塵集 — beautiful-but-free; the 薔薇 anecdote

**Verdict: CONFIRMED (both the "beautiful/free" reputation and, via my own direct textual comparison, the substance of the 薔薇 anecdote)**

- General reputation as an independent, beautifying poetic recreation rather than a strict translation is well documented in publisher/bibliographic descriptions: 車塵集 (1929) is described as translations of Chinese women poets' quatrains "transcending the domain of mere poetry translation, becoming an independent work in its own right," using archaic 七五調 lyric forms, with titles invented by Satō rather than derived from the originals. 講談社BOOK倶楽部, 『車塵集・ほるとがる文』product page. https://bookclub.kodansha.co.jp/product?item=0000167662
- A concrete, citable case of philological criticism of a specific 車塵集 poem exists: economist/essayist 河上肇, in his essay 「閑人詩話」, criticizes Satō's rendering of 紀映淮's quatrain (「杏花一孤村・流水數間屋・夕陽不見人・牛麥中宿」, translated by Satō as 「水彩風景」) — specifically disputing Satō's rendering of 数間の屋 ("a house of several rooms") as 家おちこち ("scattered houses"), arguing from Tao Yuanming/Su Dongpo/Lu You usage that 間 in this context denotes rooms within one house, not multiple houses. 河上肇, 『閑人詩話』, available at Aozora Bunko. https://www.aozora.gr.jp/cards/000250/files/43688_33024.html — Note: in this specific poem the flower (杏 apricot) is translated correctly; the criticized error is about the house, not a flower.
- **The 薔薇 anecdote, checked directly:** 車塵集 contains a separate poem titled 「薔薇をつめば」("If one plucks roses"), set to music by Akutagawa Yasushi. Its text: 「きさらぎ彌生春のさかり／草と水との色はみどり／枝をたわめて薔薇をつめば／うれしき人が息の香ぞする」(text via LiederNet Archive, https://www.lieder.net/get_text.html?TextId=120225). The source poem is 《孟珠》(also transmitted as 《丹陽孟珠歌》, attributed in some traditions to the Six Dynasties poetess 鮑令暉, collected in 《樂府詩集》juan 49): 「陽春二三月，草與水同色。攀條摘香花，言是歡氣息。」(text via gushiwen.cn and multiple mirrors). Comparing the two: the Chinese original names **no specific flower species** — 香花 just means "a fragrant flower" — while Satō's Japanese supplies the specific, invented image of 薔薇 (rose). Roses do not naturally bloom in lunar February/March (綺 the season named explicitly in both texts, 陽春二三月 / きさらぎ彌生); the original's vagueness about species is exactly the kind of ambiguity a "beautifying but free" translator would resolve by inventing a concrete, pretty image rather than staying with the unspecified original. This directly matches the anecdote's shape (an early-spring/lunar-Feb-March flower rendered as 薔薇), even though I could not find a scholar who has already made this specific point in print — this is my own comparison of the two published texts, not a citation of prior criticism.
- A full monograph exists that could contain further, more explicit discussion of fidelity throughout the whole collection: 吉川発輝, 『佐藤春夫の「車塵集」―中国歴朝名媛詩の比較研究』, 新典社, 1989 (a book-length comparative study of the originals against Satō's translations) — flagged for anyone wanting to pursue this further, but I did not obtain its full text.

### 1c. 井伏鱒二 (Ibuse Masuji) — free renderings, e.g. 「サヨナラ」ダケガ人生ダ for 勧酒

**Verdict: CONFIRMED**

- Peer-reviewed/academic-adjacent source found and directly on point: 芥川敏子, 「井伏鱒二『厄除け詩集』の『勧酒』について」, 『文学と教育』no. 166 (1994), pp. 53–57. DOI 10.19023/bungakutokyoiku.1994.166_53. https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1390001205783119360 (full text not retrieved, but bibliographic entry confirms a dedicated academic treatment of exactly this translation.)
- Corroborating non-academic but detailed commentary: Ibuse's rendering of 于武陵's 勧酒 in 『厄除け詩集』(1937) is widely characterized as going "beyond free translation, close to an original creative poem based on the Chinese source" (「意訳をこえ、漢詩をもとにした創作詩に近い」), citing his substitution of the vessel 金屈巵 with the everyday word さかずき and his colloquial, spoken-theatrical rendering of the final line. Multiple Japanese literary-appreciation blogs/sites converge on this description (note.com, koten.sk46.com, ameblo.jp), and the phrase 「サヨナラ」ダケガ人生ダ is repeatedly noted as more famous in Japan than the original poem itself.

## 2. Haiku → Chinese

### 2a. 陳黎/張芬齡 (Chen Li & Chang Fen-ling) — reputed low quality

**Verdict: UNSUPPORTED (no scholarly/professional criticism found) — weak, non-scholarly corroboration only**

- Extensive searching (Chinese, English) turned up no scholarly article, professional book review, or literary-critical essay criticizing the fidelity/quality of Chen Li and Chang Fen-ling's haiku translations (e.g. 《一茶三百句：小林一茶經典俳句選》, 2018; 《紫潮：日據時期花蓮短歌·俳句選》, 2012). Their public reputation as translators is generally presented positively (they hold Taiwan's 梁實秋文學獎翻譯獎 and 胡適翻譯獎).
- The only critical material found is informal reader review commentary on Douban for 《一茶三百句》(douban.com/subject/30466715): a reviewer using the handle "求诚斋" states bluntly that "the translator's level is really not good" and contrasts the translation unfavorably with Zhou Zuoren's earlier haiku renderings; another reviewer ("一粒家田米") argues haiku is inherently too compact to translate at all. These are public, dated, attributable comments but are **reader reviews, not published scholarly or professional criticism** — I flag them only as a data point, not as confirmation. The book's aggregate Douban rating is high (8.6/10, 91% four-or-five-star).
- I did not find evidence of a plagiarism/copyright dispute or any other documented controversy involving this pair.

### 2b. 周作人 (Zhou Zuoren) — high-fidelity renderer of Japanese poetry/haiku

**Verdict: CONFIRMED**

- Zhou Zuoren is documented as the earliest Chinese translator/populizer of haiku (his 1916 essay 《日本之俳句》) and explicitly stated his own translation principle as fidelity-first: "我的翻译，重在忠实的传达原文的意思，但一方面在形式上也并不忽略，仍然期望保存本来的若干的风格" (1918 statement; reiterated in his 1925 《陀螺序》, where he states direct/literal translation — 直译 — is the best method provided it "conveys the meaning" 达意). Cited in 刘军, 「周作人与日本文学翻译」, 《鲁迅研究月刊》2005年第6期. https://library.ttcdw.com/uploadfiles/201403/1395526069.pdf
- His translation of Ishikawa Takuboku's tanka collections is specifically praised as still being regarded as the best Chinese rendering of Takuboku's poetry ("至今仍被视为汉译日文诗中的最佳之作"), and his Kobayashi Issa and Bashō translations are described as retaining strong 诗趣 (poetic flavor) while staying close to the source. See also 北京日报, 「周作人最早翻译和介绍日本俳句，松尾芭蕉不止有'池蛙'」. https://bj.bjd.com.cn/5b165687a010550e5ddc0e6a/contentShare/5b1a1310e4b03aa54d764016/AP5c94c6efe4b099b8d059ddb6.html
- Notably, this is also the comparison point used by the Douban reviewer criticizing Chen Li/Chang Fen-ling above — i.e., Zhou Zuoren's high-fidelity reputation is being actively invoked by readers as the standard against which more recent haiku translators are found wanting.

## 3. Shakespeare sonnets → Japanese

### 3a. 坪内逍遥 (Tsubouchi Shōyō) — oldest complete Shakespeare; domesticating style

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED (in part) — mixed finding, reported as such rather than forced**

- Confirmed he did translate the Sonnets specifically, as part of his one-man complete works project: 「詩篇其二」(Sonnets), published as vol. 39 of the revised complete Shakespeare, Chūōkōron-sha, 1934 (project ran 1909's Hamlet through this 1928/1934 volume). Available at Aozora Bunko/Wikisource.
- On general style: rather than being more "domesticating" than later translators, several sources state the **opposite** at the level of sentence structure — his translation is described as having comparatively little paraphrase or word-order rearrangement, following the English grammar/word order fairly directly ("現代のシェイクスピア翻訳家と比べると意訳や語順の入れ替えが少なく…原文を英語文法のまま素直に訳されている"), with no added sentences not in the original. Later translator Matsuoka Kazuko is noted as always consulting Shōyō's version specifically to avoid mistranslation. See the Kwansei Gakuin repository paper on "坪内逍遥とシェイクスピアの『不思議』" (bibliographic entry located; full text not extractable — corrupted PDF) and general commentary collected via web search.
- However, at the level of **diction/register**, his translation is unambiguously domesticating in a different sense: he renders Shakespeare in classical Japanese 文語体 modeled on Kabuki dialogue ("歌舞伎の台詞に基づいた文語体"), which is a strong cultural/stylistic domestication even if the syntax tracks the English closely. So: "domesticating" is true of his diction/theatrical register, false (or at least contested) if it's meant to describe free paraphrase of content. I report this as a mixed/contradicted finding rather than picking a side artificially.

### 3b. 高松雄一 (Takamatsu Yūichi) — reputed good/faithful sonnets translation

**Verdict: UNSUPPORTED**

- I could not find a substantive scholarly or professional review assessing the fidelity of Takamatsu's 1986 Iwanami Bunko translation of the Sonnets. A Shūkan Shinchō piece (渡部昇一, Feb 11, 2016) discusses the Sonnets' literary merit but does not evaluate Takamatsu's translation choices. https://www.bookbang.jp/review/article/507641
- Reader-level commentary (Bookmeter, various blogs) describes the translation as "文語体で美しく、ロマンチック" (beautiful and romantic in classical style) and includes hedged praise like "高松さんの翻訳もすばらしいのだろうと思う" ("I imagine Takamatsu's translation is also wonderful too") — this is impressionistic reader reaction, not a documented critical assessment of fidelity. No negative commentary was found either.
- Bottom line: reputation as "good" is plausible from the fact that his is the standard Iwanami Bunko edition still in print, but I found no citable source that actually assesses or argues for its fidelity.
